Hand detection uses MediaPipe Hands; voice harmonization uses Signalsmith Stretch, a formant-preserving pitch shifter, with Tone.js's simpler shifter as an automatic fallback if that engine can't load — both run entirely in your browser; no audio or video leaves your device. Formant preservation is what keeps a shifted voice sounding like a voice instead of a chipmunked/robotic copy of yours: naive pitch-shifting drags your vocal-tract resonances up or down along with the pitch, which is most of what reads as "computerized." Each voice also gets a few milliseconds of independent, slowly-wandering delay on top of a tiny detune, the same trick that makes doubled/stacked human backing vocals sound like separate people rather than one voice copied — this part isn't dependent on the shifter and helps either way. None of this fully closes the gap with dedicated hardware harmonizer rigs (the kind behind Imogen Heap's or Ariana Grande's live setups), but it's a real step closer, not just a tuning tweak. Gesture logic: your index, middle, ring, and pinky fingers each independently control one harmony voice (Harmony A/B/C/D respectively, matching the colors in the panel) — raise any combination of them to bring in exactly those voices; lower a finger and its voice drops back out. Your thumb isn't used. Each harmony voice sits at a fixed semitone offset from your live voice, set by its own slider — drag one down while you sing to hear it move in real time, and use "Test the 4 harmony voices" to preview the current settings on a held tone regardless of your hand. The formant-preserving engine is heavier on your CPU than the old shifter, so if audio gets choppier than before, try the "Choir polish" toggle off (if it's on) and close other tabs/apps first. If it still stutters even with every finger down (no harmonies), that's almost always outside the app itself — most commonly a Bluetooth headset (AirPods and similar) dropping to its low-bandwidth call-quality mode the moment it's also supplying the microphone, or the browser tab running low on CPU. Wired headphones/mic, or a wired USB mic, sidestep the Bluetooth issue entirely.
